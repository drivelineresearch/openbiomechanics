# OBP Additional Resources

An annotated reference list of the books and papers that inform the OpenBiomechanics
Project's methods and conventions. The copyrighted texts that previously lived in this
folder have been removed; the entries below link to the official publisher, DOI, or
journal pages so you can obtain each work through legitimate channels.

## Books

- **Winter, D.A. (2009). *Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement*, 4th ed.
  Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.**
  The standard reference for the mechanics of human movement: anthropometrics, kinematics,
  inverse dynamics, EMG, and signal processing of biomechanical data.
  <https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Biomechanics+and+Motor+Control+of+Human+Movement%2C+4th+Edition-p-9780470398180>

- **Zatsiorsky, V.M. (2000). *Biomechanics in Sport: Performance Enhancement and Injury
  Prevention*. Oxford: Wiley-Blackwell.**
  Applied sport biomechanics with a focus on performance and injury mechanisms — useful
  context for interpreting the pitching and hitting signals in this dataset.
  <https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Biomechanics+in+Sport%3A+Performance+Enhancement+and+Injury+Prevention-p-9780632053926>

- **McCaw, S.T. (2014). *Biomechanics for Dummies*. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.**
  An accessible, plain-language introduction to biomechanics concepts for readers new to
  the field.
  <https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Biomechanics+For+Dummies-p-9781118674697>

- **Kinder, J.M. & Nelson, P. (2015). *A Student's Guide to Python for Physical Modeling*
  (Elementary Mechanics Using Python). Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press /
  Springer.**
  A practical guide to modeling classical mechanics problems in Python — a good bridge
  between the physics and the code used to process motion-capture data.
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-19596-4>

## Papers — ISB joint coordinate system standards

The two Wu et al. ISB recommendations below are the standards that this project's joint
coordinate system conventions build on.

- **Wu, G., Siegler, S., Allard, P., et al. (2002). ISB recommendation on definitions of
  joint coordinate system of various joints for the reporting of human joint motion — part
  I: ankle, hip, and spine. *Journal of Biomechanics*, 35(4), 543–548.**
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9290(01)00222-6>

- **Wu, G., van der Helm, F.C.T., Veeger, H.E.J., et al. (2005). ISB recommendation on
  definitions of joint coordinate systems of various joints for the reporting of human
  joint motion — Part II: shoulder, elbow, wrist and hand. *Journal of Biomechanics*,
  38(5), 981–992.**
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2004.05.042>
